# Product-Market Fit (PMF) for Indie Builders: Identification & Measurement

> **A first-principles guide to understanding, identifying, measuring, and achieving Product-Market Fit (PMF) as an independent creator or bootstrapped startup.**

---

## 1. What is Product-Market Fit (PMF)?

**Product-Market Fit (PMF)** occurs when a product satisfies a strong market demand with a scalable, repeatable value proposition. Coined by Marc Andreessen, PMF means *"being in a good market with a product that can satisfy that market."*

## 2. Key Signals of Product-Market Fit

### A. The Sean Ellis 40% Rule
Survey your active users with a single question:  
*"How would you feel if you could no longer use [Product Name]?"*
- Very disappointed
- Somewhat disappointed
- Not disappointed

> **PMF Benchmark**: If **40% or more** of respondents answer *"Very disappointed"*, you have achieved strong Product-Market Fit.

### B. Flattening Retention Curves
Plot user cohort retention over 30, 60, and 90 days. If the retention curve flattens parallel to the x-axis (e.g., retaining 20%-30% of users indefinitely), you have stable retention.

### C. Organic Word-of-Mouth & Pull Demand
Users actively recommend your tool to colleagues without referral incentives. Inbound feedback shifts from *"Can you build feature X?"* to *"When will feature Y be ready? I need this now."*

---

## 3. Navigating "Fake PMF" Traps

- ❌ **Spike from Viral Launch**: High initial signups driven by Product Hunt or Hacker News do not equal PMF if 95% of users churn within 7 days.
- ❌ **Vanity Signups**: Free user signups without usage engagement or conversion to paid tiers.
- ❌ **Discount-Driven Conversion**: Users buying only because of heavy 90% lifetime discounts rather than core product utility.

---

## 4. Summary

Product-Market Fit is not a binary switch—it is a continuous spectrum. Until you hit PMF, every engineering resource should focus exclusively on user retention, problem validation, and core feature refinement.
