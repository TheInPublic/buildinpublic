# Unit Economics for Bootstrapped Software: CAC, LTV, Margin & Payback Period

> **A first-principles guide to financial unit economics, contribution margins, CAC payback periods, and the Rule of 40 for bootstrapped software products.**

---

## 1. What are Unit Economics?

**Unit Economics** measures the direct revenues and costs associated with a single unit of production—in software, a single active user or paying customer. Understanding unit economics determines whether a software business will compound profit or burn money as it scales.

```text
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                      THE UNIT ECONOMICS HEALTH CHECK                      │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
   Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)  ──► Must be at least 3x Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
   CAC Payback Period             ──► Must recover acquisition cost in under 12 months
   Gross Margin %                 ──► SaaS target: 70% to 90%+
```

---

## 2. Core Financial Metrics & Formulas

### A. Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)
The total gross profit generated by a customer over their entire relationship with your software.

$$\text{LTV} = \frac{\text{ARPU} \times \text{Gross Margin \%}}{\text{Monthly Churn Rate}}$$

### B. Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
The total money spent on marketing, advertising, tools, and sales divided by the number of new customers acquired during that period.

$$\text{CAC} = \frac{\text{Total Acquisition Spend}}{\text{New Customers Acquired}}$$

### C. CAC Payback Period (Months to Cost Recovery)
The number of months required for a customer to generate enough gross profit to cover their initial acquisition cost.

$$\text{CAC Payback Period (Months)} = \frac{\text{CAC}}{\text{ARPU} \times \text{Gross Margin \%}}$$

> **Bootstrapped Benchmark**: Aim for a CAC Payback Period of **under 6 months** (maximum 12 months) to maintain healthy cash flow without external funding.

---

## 3. The Rule of 40 for SaaS Health

The **Rule of 40** is a classic metric used to evaluate the overall performance and sustainability of a SaaS business:

$$\text{Annual Revenue Growth Rate \%} + \text{Free Cash Flow Margin \%} \ge 40\%$$

*Examples*:
- A startup growing at **30% YoY** with a **15% profit margin** score is $30 + 15 = 45\%$ (**Healthy**).
- A bootstrapped SaaS with **10% growth** but a **35% profit margin** score is $10 + 35 = 45\%$ (**Healthy**).

---

## 4. Summary

Mastering unit economics provides financial clarity. When your LTV is significantly higher than your CAC and your payback period is short, every new user directly compounds your business runway and profitability.
